# Mission and constraints

## Mission
Find a real, recurring business problem; validate demand before committing to a product; build a useful solution with AI-assisted development; acquire customers responsibly; and document the entire process with evidence.

## Goals
- Execution begins: 1 February 2027, after Semester 1 exams.
- Stretch goal: ₹1 crore in cumulative revenue actually collected by 31 December 2027.
- Learning goals: practical software engineering, customer discovery, sales, product judgment, operations, finance and responsible AI use.
- Career goal: build real-world proof of execution while continuing B.Tech studies and internship preparation.

## Constraints
- Starting capital: ₹0.
- Hardware: HP laptop, 10th-generation Intel i3, 4 GB RAM. Prefer lightweight web tools, hosted development environments where free/available, and modest local workloads.
- Development: Codex and AI-assisted tools may accelerate work, but generated code must be understood, reviewed, tested and secured.
- Research: Gemini Argon 4 Task Mode may support research and lead generation; independently verify facts, sources and contact information.
- Sales: first-time founder; no assumed founder network or prior selling experience.
- Education: business execution must not displace essential studies, sleep, or health.
- Legal/compliance: age, business structure, taxation, data protection, telecom and commercial-calling requirements must be checked with reliable sources and qualified help when necessary.

## Financial definitions
Track separately:
- Gross invoiced revenue
- Revenue collected
- Refunds/credits
- Operating expenses and usage costs
- Taxes and statutory liabilities
- Gross margin and net profit
- Cash available to reinvest
- Founder withdrawals

₹1 crore collected revenue is a stretch target, not a forecast or promise. Never report revenue as personal profit.

## Operating principles
1. Validate before building.
2. Talk about the customer's existing behaviour, not just opinions about an idea.
3. Prefer the smallest test that can disprove a hypothesis.
4. Build only the smallest workflow needed to test the value.
5. Do not automate high-risk actions before reliability and human approval are established.
6. Protect private and customer information.
7. Record negative findings and pivots.
8. Make decisions using evidence, not sunk-cost attachment.
9. Prefer repeatable recurring value over one-off novelty.
10. Review progress weekly and adjust workload around college commitments.
