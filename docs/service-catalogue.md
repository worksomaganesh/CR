## 1. Purpose and positioning

CR aims to be an **AI-native technical-solutions company**: identify real business problems, choose appropriate technologies, build and verify practical solutions with AI-assisted delivery, deploy them responsibly, and earn revenue by delivering measurable value.

CR is not restricted to chatbots, one RAG architecture, or one industry. Its toolbox may include AI models, RAG/retrieval variants, agents, APIs, workflow automation, document processing, data systems, analytics, custom software, ML, multimodal systems, and newer technologies where they solve an evidenced problem.

**We sell business outcomes, not technology names.** The portfolio is intentionally broad; the active offer at any time must be narrow. Start with a specific buyer, recurring workflow, measurable outcome and credible path to a paid pilot. This catalogue is not proof of demand or a commitment to launch every service.

## 2. Service lines and offerings

### A. AI marketing and business growth

**Potential buyers:** Small/growing businesses, local service providers, and organisations needing a repeatable content or campaign workflow.

- **Social media posts and business ad creatives:** branded static creatives, post concepts, carousel outlines and platform-appropriate formats.
- **Reel ideas:** concepts, hooks, outlines and optional scripts/storyboards. Filming, voiceovers, editing and full video production are separately scoped.
- **Captions and hashtags:** platform-appropriate variants and relevant hashtag suggestions; no promises of reach or virality.
- **Monthly campaign calendar:** planned posting themes, campaign dates, content types and calls to action.
- **WhatsApp promotional content:** copy and campaign variants for legitimate, permission-based business communication. Sending or bulk distribution is not included by default.
- **Google review replies:** draft responses to genuine reviews; no fake reviews or undisclosed manipulation.
- **Monthly competitor analysis:** research from accessible public sources, with dates, source links, observed claims and a clear distinction between evidence and inference.
- **AI marketing suggestions:** testable recommendations based on customer context and available metrics; label hypotheses separately from measured outcomes.
- **Optional performance reporting:** agreed metrics only where reliable data access exists.

**Possible commercial shape:** Monthly content/campaign package plus optional competitor research or performance reporting. Define content quantity, revision limits, platforms, approval process and whether scheduling/publishing/community management is included. Content creation alone is not a promise of leads or revenue.

### B. AI knowledge infrastructure

**Potential buyers:** Teams whose approved information is spread across PDFs, manuals, policies, shared documents or multiple internal sources.

- **AI knowledge infrastructure:** organize, index, retrieve and maintain approved business knowledge.
- **AI FAQ assistant:** answer common customer/staff questions using approved sources and handle missing evidence clearly.
- **PDF chatbot:** question answering over supplied PDFs, with source references where feasible.
- **Employee knowledge AI:** internal assistant for permitted procedures, manuals, onboarding and policies.
- **Internal business knowledge system:** search across approved sources with permissions appropriate to document sensitivity.

Possible components include document parsing, chunking, embeddings, keyword/vector/hybrid retrieval, reranking, source-grounded generation, access control, evaluation and update/deletion workflows. Use knowledge graphs, agentic retrieval, multimodal processing or persistent memory only when a demonstrated need justifies the extra complexity. Define source updates, unsupported-question handling and access permissions.

**Possible commercial shape:** Scoped implementation plus optional source maintenance, evaluation, monitoring and support. Never promise perfect accuracy; expose sources/uncertainty and require human approval for consequential decisions.

### C. AI sales infrastructure

**Potential buyers:** Businesses that manage enquiries, qualify opportunities, prepare proposals/quotes and need consistent follow-up.

- **AI proposal generator:** draft proposals from approved templates, business facts and customer inputs.
- **AI quote generator:** prepare quotation drafts from approved product/service catalogues and pricing rules.
- **AI lead qualification:** classify enquiries against customer-approved criteria, capture missing fields and route leads appropriately.
- **Sales reporting:** track enquiries, qualification, follow-ups, proposals/quotes and outcomes where source data supports the metrics.

Final prices, discounts, contractual language, commitments and external sending need an authorised human approval step unless a separately validated control process permits automation. Never invent pricing, capabilities or customer facts.

**Possible commercial shape:** Setup/pilot fee plus optional maintenance and clearly disclosed usage charges.

### D. AI customer operations

**Potential buyers:** Businesses receiving recurring enquiries, booking requests or support questions.

- **AI WhatsApp employee:** approved FAQ handling, enquiry capture, workflow actions and human handoff through permitted integrations.
- **AI receptionist:** supported calls/chats, business-information responses, message capture and routing.
- **AI customer support:** knowledge-grounded responses, triage, ticket classification and draft replies.
- **AI appointment assistant:** availability checks, booking requests, confirmations, reminders and rescheduling subject to real calendar permissions and business rules.

A feature name is not a promise of full autonomy. Define supported languages, business hours, failure paths, escalation, booking/transaction permissions, conversation retention, provider/API limits and monitoring. Verify current WhatsApp, telephony, consent, commercial-communication and vendor requirements before launch. A provider does not automatically make a campaign compliant.

**Possible commercial shape:** Implementation plus recurring managed-service fees and transparent usage/third-party charges.

### E. Websites, lead capture and integrations

**Potential buyers:** Businesses needing a focused online presence or less manual transfer between existing tools.

- **Business websites and landing pages:** focused service pages, campaign pages, contact pages and clear calls to action.
- **Lead capture forms:** validated forms with approved fields and privacy information where appropriate.
- **Basic business integrations:** Google Forms, Google Sheets, Google Calendar, WhatsApp Business and other supported tools/APIs where customer accounts, permissions and provider terms allow.
- **Workflow automation:** notifications, approvals, data transfer, scheduled tasks and repetitive processes.
- **Internal dashboards/lightweight tools:** focused utilities where an existing spreadsheet or SaaS feature is insufficient.

Prefer existing low-cost tools and standard connectors before custom software. State what hosting, domain, platform fees, maintenance and content updates are included.

**Possible commercial shape:** Fixed-scope build/setup plus optional maintenance, hosting administration or integration support.

### F. Business reporting and data analytics

**Potential buyers:** Businesses needing clearer operational or commercial decisions from existing data.

- **Basic reporting:** structured Excel/CSV/Sheets outputs, data cleaning, KPI summaries and charts.
- **Monthly performance report:** agreed metrics and trends with data source, date range, limitations and actionable observations.
- **Analytics dashboard:** interactive KPI visuals, filters, drilldowns and a data model where justified.
- **Power BI solution:** suitable data model, queries, measures, visuals, validation and documented handover; a Power BI Desktop project file (.pbix) can be one deliverable.
- **Automated reporting:** recurring data refresh/report generation where source access, APIs, licensing and hosting support it.
- **Advanced analytics:** segmentation, anomaly detection, forecasting or other analysis only when data quality, validation and business value justify the method.

#### Choose the deliverable by requirements, not prestige

| Level | Suitable when | Typical deliverable | Important limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Excel / Google Sheets | One/few structured sources; simple reporting; downloadable output is sufficient | Clean workbook, formulas/pivots, KPI summary and charts | May need manual refresh; validate source data and formulas |
| Power BI Desktop / PBIX | Related tables, reusable measures, modeled data or interactive filters add value | Project file, model/measures, report pages and handover notes | Confirm the customer can open and maintain it; a Desktop file is not hosted sharing |
| Published/shared Power BI | Online access, collaboration or refresh is needed | Published report/workspace, configured data access and refresh where supported | Verify current licensing, capacity, identity, gateway and source constraints before quoting |
| Managed analytics | Reports need ongoing refresh, monitoring, interpretation or support | Agreed reporting cadence, monitoring and monthly insights | Requires defined ownership, permissions, failure handling, service levels and recurring costs |

Power BI may provide more structured modeling and sharing than a static spreadsheet/chart, but it is not automatically better or worth more for every task. An AI-generated dashboard may fit some needs but does not automatically provide Power BI's semantic modeling, refresh governance, permissions or hosted sharing. Choose against written acceptance criteria, not tool prestige.

The value is trustworthy data, agreed KPI definitions, correct joins/calculations, useful interpretation, repeatable refresh and maintainability—not simply the file extension. Quote separately for data cleaning, modeling, analysis, visualisation, automation, deployment and support when scope/risk differs. Before publication or sharing, check the customer's actual licensing and access needs using current official Microsoft documentation.

**Possible commercial shape:** One-time reporting/analytics project plus separately scoped recurring refresh, monthly analysis or dashboard support.

### G. Custom AI engineering and technical solutions

This is the cross-cutting capability line, not an unbounded promise to build anything. Potential solutions include:

- Custom AI agents and bounded multi-step workflows.
- RAG, hybrid retrieval, corrective/evaluated retrieval, knowledge graphs, memory and multimodal retrieval.
- Document/data extraction, validation, reconciliation and exception-review workflows.
- API integrations, internal apps, backend services, databases and dashboards.
- ML classification, forecasting, anomaly detection or computer vision where data and evaluation support them.
- Testing, model/output evaluation, AI guardrails, monitoring and deployment support.
- Future technology-driven solutions, including robotics or IoT, only when buyer demand, feasibility, safety and delivery capacity are credible.

Start with the minimum reliable architecture. Naïve, Advanced, Modular, Agentic, Graph, Self, Corrective, Hybrid, Multimodal, Memory-Augmented and Speculative RAG are not a mandatory checklist and should not be marketed as standalone services without a clear customer outcome.

## 4. Qualification and solution-design checklist

Before committing to delivery, document:

1. **Buyer and user:** who pays, who uses it, and who approves access or changes?
2. **Current workflow:** what happens today, how often, how long it takes and where errors occur?
3. **Consequence:** time, cost, missed revenue, delay, quality or risk affected.
4. **Existing alternatives:** software, built-in features, spreadsheets, staff, consultants and competing offers.
5. **Specific gap:** why those alternatives are insufficient; separate customer evidence from assumptions.
6. **Desired outcome:** baseline, success metric and measurement method.
7. **Scope:** inputs/outputs, integrations, volume, users, acceptance criteria, exclusions and timeline.
8. **Data and risk:** permissions, sensitivity, retention/deletion, third-party processing, prompt injection and human review.
9. **Feasibility:** tool access, reliability, latency, security, capacity and fallback.
10. **Economics:** implementation effort, API/model usage, platform/licence costs, hosting, support, tax/payment costs and expected margin.
11. **Commercial commitment:** budget authority, paid pilot terms, payment milestones and customer responsibilities.
12. **Support and ownership:** failure handling, credentials, updates, source changes, incident response and exit/export.

Do not start the full build until problem and scope justify it. A narrow paid service-assisted pilot may be better than a polished standalone product.

## 5. Standard delivery lifecycle

1. **Discover:** understand the real workflow and decision-maker.
2. **Validate:** verify pain, competitive gap, access, feasibility and willingness to pay.
3. **Specify:** define scope, exclusions, data flow, acceptance tests and success metrics.
4. **Prototype:** use synthetic/redacted data and the smallest useful workflow.
5. **Build:** AI-assisted coding with version control, bounded tool access and review.
6. **Evaluate:** deterministic tests, representative and failure cases, security/privacy checks, quality and cost measurements.
7. **Pilot and accept:** test against agreed criteria; record limitations and corrections.
8. **Deploy:** staged release, least-privilege configuration, backups, monitoring and rollback.
9. **Support:** manage incidents, updates, costs, access, deletion and service levels.
10. **Measure and reuse:** compare outcomes to baseline; reuse components only when the fit is sound.

Never report a test as passed or deployment as complete without evidence. AI-generated code or content remains a draft until appropriately verified.

## 6. Marketing, data and automation boundaries

- No fake reviews, fabricated customer results, unsupported claims, guaranteed virality or guaranteed sales/lead increases.
- Competitor analysis uses legitimate public sources, dates findings and separates observation from inference. Do not copy protected creative work.
- Respect marketing consent, platform rules, opt-outs and applicable Indian privacy/commercial communication requirements. Verify current rules before automated messaging or calling.
- Do not send confidential customer data to an AI provider before checking permission, retention, training use, region, access and deletion terms.
- Customer-facing AI must represent capabilities honestly, avoid inventing business policies/prices, and provide human handoff for unresolved or consequential cases.
- Financial, contractual, legal, medical, compliance, payment, booking cancellation and other consequential actions require appropriate approval and permissions.
- Never store secrets or sensitive customer files in GitHub. Use least privilege, suitable logging, deletion/export and rollback.
- The founder remains accountable for scope, customer promises, security, compliance decisions, production approval and delivery.

## 7. Pricing and recurring revenue principles

Do not publish a universal price card before validating segment, scope, competition, value and delivery cost. Estimate:

- Implementation and support time.
- Model/API usage, hosting, storage and monitoring.
- Third-party connector, messaging, voice, data and licence charges.
- Setup, data cleaning, onboarding, testing and deployment.
- Security, privacy, maintenance, incident handling and support.
- Payment fees, applicable taxes/statutory obligations and contingency.

Define one-time implementation, recurring maintenance/managed service, usage charges and customer-owned subscriptions/licences separately. Do not hide third-party costs or promise unlimited usage without a cost model. A monthly retainer needs a defined recurring output and response boundary.

Track invoiced revenue separately from cleared collections, refunds, direct delivery costs, operating expenses, tax liabilities, margin and personal take-home.

## 8. Prioritisation rules

Score candidate workflows using evidence for pain frequency/consequence, reachable buyer, competitive gap, willingness to pay, feasibility, time to first value, repeatability, margin potential, support burden, data/security/legal risk and opportunity cost.

Prefer opportunities where a decision-maker is reachable, current pain can be described precisely, alternatives leave a demonstrable gap, a small pilot can test value quickly and safely, implementation can be paid for, and similar customers may have the same need.

Do not select an offer only because AI makes a demo easy. Interest, a lead, a compliment or a waitlist is not willingness to pay. Keep dated records of tests and reasons to stop or pivot.
