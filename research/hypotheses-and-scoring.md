## Weighted rubric (score each candidate 1–5)
| Criterion | Weight | Score guidance |
|---|---:|---|
| Problem severity and frequency | 20% | 1 = rare/minor; 5 = frequent, costly, urgent |
| Evidence of willingness to pay | 20% | 1 = no evidence; 5 = repeated paid commitments |
| Differentiation and competition | 20% | 1 = commodity/crowded; 5 = evidenced unmet need |
| Ability to reach decision-makers | 15% | 1 = inaccessible; 5 = repeatable, legitimate channel |
| Solo-founder build feasibility | 15% | 1 = requires extensive integrations/capital; 5 = narrow MVP feasible |
| Cost, trust, privacy and regulatory risk | 10% | 1 = severe/manageability poor; 5 = low and manageable |

Weighted total = sum(score × weight) / 5, expressed as a percentage. Unknowns must be scored conservatively, not optimistically.

## Initial qualitative assessment
Do not assign a confident numerical winner until evidence is comparable. Current qualitative status:
- H1: ecosystem and incident signals exist; buyer pain, gap and willingness to pay unknown.
- H2: significant competitor overlap; a narrow exception might exist but is not yet identified.
- H3: generic functionality is already available; needs a sharply defined segment/exception.
- H4: defer as initial product due to competition, usage costs and outreach compliance complexity.

## Required disproof questions
For every candidate:
1. What does the target buyer do today?
2. What current tool/service already solves this?
3. What specific task remains painful despite that solution?
4. What evidence shows the pain is frequent and expensive?
5. Who owns the budget?
6. Can we reach the buyer legally and affordably?
7. What is the smallest paid test?
8. What evidence would make us abandon the idea?
