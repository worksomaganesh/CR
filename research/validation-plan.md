# Market validation plan

**Status:** Not yet executed with customers.  
**Owner:** Founder.  
**Start:** Desk research now; execution/customer discovery planned from 1 February 2027 unless early permission-based discovery is appropriate.

## Phase A — Secondary research
- [ ] Verify official market figures and publication dates.
- [ ] Map official portal features and current workflows.
- [ ] Compare at least five direct competitors/substitutes for the leading hypothesis.
- [ ] Capture features, published pricing, integrations, buyer segment and limitations with URLs.
- [ ] Run adversarial research in Gemini Argon 4 Task Mode.
- [ ] Verify every useful claim independently.

## Phase B — Prospect research
- [ ] Define a narrow ideal customer profile (ICP).
- [ ] Find 20 plausible businesses using public official/business sources.
- [ ] For each, record relevance evidence and a question to test—not a presumed pain.
- [ ] Use only published business contact channels; no private personal data collection.
- [ ] Manually verify all AI-generated details.

## Phase C — Customer discovery
Target: 10–15 neutral conversations, if feasible. This is a working sample target, not a statistically representative survey.

- [ ] Ask respondents to walk through a recent real workflow.
- [ ] Identify tasks, tools, people, time, failure cases and consequences.
- [ ] Ask what they already pay for and what they have tried.
- [ ] Request redacted examples only with explicit permission.
- [ ] Record exact evidence separately from interpretation.
- [ ] Do not pitch until the current process is understood.

## Phase D — Solution test
- [ ] Create a manual mock-up or low-cost prototype only after recurring pain is visible.
- [ ] Test one order/document workflow using synthetic or redacted data.
- [ ] Verify every extracted deadline/specification against source evidence.
- [ ] Measure time, error rate, corrections and user comprehension.
- [ ] Ask for a concrete paid-pilot commitment, not a vague expression of interest.

## Working decision thresholds
Continue to a small prototype only if several independent target users describe the same recurring problem, at least a few can show a real workflow/example, and there is credible evidence that current alternatives leave an important gap. A paid-pilot commitment is stronger evidence than compliments or waitlist signups.

These thresholds are internal decision rules, not universal market benchmarks. If evidence is weak, revise the ICP, change the hypothesis, or stop.

## Interview question bank
1. Walk me through the last time you completed this process.
2. What happened first, next, and last?
3. Who did each step?
4. Which tools or spreadsheets did you use?
5. What was the slowest or most error-prone step?
6. Tell me about the last time it went wrong.
7. What happened as a result?
8. How often has this occurred in the last six months?
9. What have you tried already?
10. What do you pay for the current process, including staff/consultants?
11. What would make a new tool too risky or inconvenient?
12. Can you show a redacted example, if permitted?
13. What would you need to see before considering a paid pilot?

Avoid leading questions such as “Would you use my AI app?” Record actual past behaviour first.
